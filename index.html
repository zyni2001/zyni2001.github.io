About

Hi! I'm a CS PhD student at UC Berkeley, advised by Prof. Pierluigi Nuzzo. I work on neuro-symbolic AI and AI security — getting LLMs to translate language into logic a solver can check, and stress-testing the defenses we build around them.

Before Berkeley I spent two and a half years as a PhD student at the University of Southern California (2022–2024), and earned my B.S. in Physics from the University of Science and Technology of China as an Outstanding Graduate (top 3%). This year I'm a Research Scientist Intern at Meta.

If you're interested in reasoning, formal verification, or AI security, reach out — I'm always happy to collaborate.

02

Research

Three threads, one question: can we trust what a model concludes?

∀∃

Auto-formalization & Logical Reasoning

Turning natural-language arguments into executable first-order logic, then letting a solver do the reasoning. Drafting many formalizations, pruning the contradictory ones, and keeping what verifies.

  • FOL
  • Z3
  • FOLIO
  • AR-LSAT
⊢

Agents with Guarantees

Agentic systems that come with something to check — assessor agents with structured failure tracking, and self-evolving contract frameworks for verified controller synthesis in cyber-physical systems.

  • Agents
  • Contracts
  • CPS
  • Verification
¬◊

AI Security & Robustness

Showing where defenses learn shortcuts instead of intent, building adversarially robust sparse GNNs, and secure cloud–device learning that keeps the informative features encrypted.

  • Prompt attacks
  • Adversarial GNNs
  • Privacy

Publications

* equal contribution  ·  ♦ equal advising  ·  click a paper for highlights

  1. An agentic framework where contracts evolve alongside the synthesized controller, so every generated controller comes with a specification it can be verified against.

  2. Controlled diagnostics reveal position, token-trigger, and topic-generalization shortcuts in fine-tuned prompt-attack defenses. High rejection rates often come from surface correlations, not from understanding harmful intent.

    • ↑50% false refusals
    • ↓40% accuracy under shift

  3. An inference-time framework that drafts diverse plans, prunes contradictory or ambiguous formalizations, and aggregates verified paths, with no extra supervision.

    • 78.43% AR-LSAT
    • 100% PrOntoQA
    • 100% LogicalDeduction
    arXiv ↗

  4. A reproducible assessor-agent framework with structured failure tracking and standardized agent-to-agent interfaces, plus an auto-formalization agent that compiles natural-language logic into executable Z3Py under auditable budgets.

    • 86.70% cleaned FOLIO
    • vs 73.89% chain-of-thought
    arXiv ↗Workshop ↗

  5. A secure cloud–device framework that combines hypernetwork-based personalization with selective feature encryption. It matches full fine-tuning without backpropagation on the device, and dynamic feature sampling encrypts only the informative channels.

  6. GrokTransfer initializes a target model with embeddings learned by a weaker model. It provably generalizes directly on synthetic XOR and removes delayed generalization in MLPs and Transformers across tasks.

    OpenReview ↗

  7. ARGS jointly prunes perturbed graph edges and GNN weights with label- and homophily-aware objectives. The result is highly sparse GNNs with much stronger adversarial robustness and lower inference cost.

    OpenReview ↗Code ↗

  8. The first systematic look at how sparse graph lottery tickets hold up under structural attacks, which motivated the ARGS defense.

    Session ↗Slides ↗
